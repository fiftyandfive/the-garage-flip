How to prepare for an
estate garage cleanout.

There is no version of this that is only logistics. A short, practical guide for families clearing a garage after a loss or a move — at whatever pace feels right.

Get a fixed quote Call (407) 735-6450

It was never clutter. It is a life.

You do not have to decide everything at once, and you do not have to be the one to open the door.

Estate garages are different from ordinary ones. They hold tools someone used every Saturday for thirty years, boxes nobody has opened since a move in the nineties, and — often — documents and valuables the family did not know were there.

The work below is what we would suggest to anyone, whether you hire us or handle it yourselves.

Before anyone starts

Four things to do first.

i.

Sweep for documents and valuables

Garages hide deeds, titles, savings bonds, cash tins, jewelry, and firearms far more often than families expect. Before any volume of items moves, walk the space specifically looking for paper, safes, lockboxes, and anything in a drawer.

ii.

Name the untouchables out loud

Decide in advance what is not going anywhere — a workbench, a toolbox, a set of golf clubs. Saying it before the sorting starts spares everyone a difficult moment mid-job.

iii.

Give family a window to claim

A short, clear deadline prevents the slow stall that leaves a garage untouched for a year. A shared photo album and a date works better than open-ended promises.

iv.

Make an "unsure" box — and honor it

Anything you are not ready to decide on goes in a box that waits for you. Nothing forces a decision on the day, and having that option makes the rest of the sorting far easier.

From afar · Documented for family
From out of state

When you cannot be in the room.

If the house is in Orlando and you are not, the work can still be done without a flight. Ask for a photo review of every keep-decision, a documented before-and-after, and a single point of contact who can reach you during the day. In most cases the estate covers the cost of the clear-out.

Our estate approach

Timing

If there is a closing date.

Probate and estate sales often put a hard date on the calendar. A full garage clear-out is usually a single day of work, so it does not need to be the thing that holds up a closing — but it does need to be booked rather than assumed.

A practical order: documents and valuables first, family claims second, clear-out third, then photos for the listing. If the home is going on the market, a cleared and swept garage also removes the last objection a buyer walks through.
